# D-0001 — A vintage motorcycle that jumps as a horse does

**The ask** (Rusty, 2026-10-23): add the vintage motorcycle model received that day to the Vanilla
Wheels roster; it jumps exactly as a ridden horse does, same controls; a cool name. The name settled
with him: the **Iron Horse**. Two seats: the rider, and a pillion on the pad behind the saddle.

**Decision**:
1. A data-only repo on Vanilla Wheels 1.19.0, which added what a bike needs (its D-0047): the horse's
   jump (`jump`, an average horse's 0.7), the lean (`lean`, at most 35 degrees, 8 left parked), parts
   of the model that move (the fork steering with the front wheel and the headlamp in it, both wheels
   rolling, the kickstand folding while ridden), and a rolling part counting as a wheel on the lift.
   Nothing here is Java but the gametests and the booth.
2. **The model is adopted as received**: kept byte for byte as the reference, fitted with a profile,
   never redrawn. The only change to the game's copy is the slate parts' texture made grey for the dye,
   with a factory colour that gives the received slate back undyed (`tools/bbgen/paint.py`).
3. **On its stand parked.** The kickstand is modelled down, on the left, its foot a little short of
   the ground upright; leaning the bike 8 degrees left onto it sets the foot on the ground to the eye.
   Ridden, the stand turns 70 degrees back about its hinge, lying along the frame.
4. **Its numbers**: a heavier, steadier machine than the Scrambler: faster flat out (0.85), slower off
   the mark, wider turns (30 degrees of lock); five steel and a leather saddle for the chassis, eight
   steel to rebuild a wreck; three quarters of a truck's tank; durability 4; a headlamp of range 10.
   No horn: Vanilla Wheels has only a truck's.

**Alternatives**: a pillion that is cargo (refused: a player rides it, as in any second seat); a
kickstand drawn down only when parked by a second, parked kind of moving part (unneeded: the model
draws it down, so folding it while ridden is the one motion).
